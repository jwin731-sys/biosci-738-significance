## Figure A — Rank-abundance of observers across seven bounded-site iNaturalist projects

**`figA_rank_abundance_all.png`**

Each panel shows the rank-ordered observation count of contributors to a bounded-site iNaturalist
project (3,400–12,200 total observations). Axes are log-log. The dashed line is a power-law fit to
ranks 2–20 (least-squares regression of log counts on log ranks); the × symbol marks where rank-1
falls on that fit (the *predicted* rank-1 count); the filled circle marks the *actual* rank-1 count.
Three projects (UoA Campus, Dye Creek Preserve, and Arkansas State University Querétaro Campus)
show a rank-1 contributor substantially above the power-law prediction — the "excess" annotated in
each panel. The remaining four projects show rank-1 contributors at or below the prediction, a
pattern consistent with a continuous power-law distribution across all ranks. Projects are
identified by institution and location; rank-1 observer identity is available in the data supplement.

## Figure B — Annual observation trajectories of three devoted single observers

**`figB_annual_trajectory.png`**

Annual count of observations submitted to a bounded-site iNaturalist project by the rank-1
contributor in the three Thorpe-like cases. Left: Stephen Thorpe at the University of Auckland
City Campus (UoA, New Zealand); red dashed line marks his last recorded observation (August 2024,
the month of his death). Centre: Scott Hardage (username *dyecreekscott*) at Dye Creek Preserve,
Tehama County, California; contributions are ongoing as of May 2026. Right: Daniel Castañeda
(username *shady_meerkat*) at the Arkansas State University Querétaro Campus, Mexico; contributions
ceased in June 2024. Note the differing y-axis scales. All three observers show multi-year
sustained contribution rather than a single-event spike, in contrast to bioblitz participants
(see Figure 3A).

## Figure C — Three quantitative signatures of the devoted-observer pattern

**`figC_thorpe_character_metrics.png`**

Comparison of three observer-heterogeneity metrics across all seven projects. Left: ratio of
rank-1 to rank-2 observation counts (R1/R2); the dashed line at 1.0 indicates equal contribution.
Centre: power-law excess — the ratio of actual rank-1 count to the count predicted by a power-law
fit to ranks 2–20; values above 1.0 indicate a rank-1 contributor beyond what the rest of the
community's distribution would predict. Right: rank-1 observer's share of all project observations.
The three Thorpe-like projects (coloured bars) show higher values on all three metrics than the
four typical campus projects (grey). The dashed line in the left and centre panels marks the
expectation under a continuous power law (R1/R2 ≈ 2^α and excess = 1.0 respectively, where α is
the fitted slope). Note that the UoA Campus project is the most extreme on R1/R2 (8.7×) and %
share (35.4%) despite its power-law excess (4.9×) being similar to Dye Creek (6.3×); this reflects
a relatively sparse community of secondary contributors around Thorpe.
